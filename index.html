📖 Position Sizing: The Trader's Edge

Most traders focus on entry and exit. But position sizing is the single most important factor in long-term profitability.

The 1% Rule

Never risk more than 1-2% of your total account on a single trade. If you have a $10,000 account, your maximum loss per trade should be $100-$200. This means if your stop loss is hit, you only lose 1-2% of your capital.

The Formula

Position Size = (Account × Risk%) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss)

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk, entry at $150, stop at $145.
Position Size = ($10,000 × 0.01) / ($150 - $145) = $100 / $5 = 20 shares

Why This Matters

  • With 1% risk, you can survive 20 consecutive losses and still have 82% of your account
  • With 5% risk, 10 consecutive losses leaves you with only 60% of your account
  • Position sizing removes emotion — you know exactly how much you can lose before entering
  • It forces you to place stops where the thesis is wrong, not where you "feel comfortable"

The Risk/Reward Filter

Only take trades with a minimum 1:2 risk/reward ratio. If you risk $100, your target should be at least $200 in potential profit. This means you can be wrong 60% of the time and still be profitable.
